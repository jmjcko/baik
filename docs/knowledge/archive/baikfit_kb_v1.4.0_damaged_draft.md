## 1. System Architecture & Datum Reference 

To ensure absolute precision across different frame geometries, saddles, and component brands, all biomechanical measurements MUST be referenced to a fixed physical datum point:

* **Datum Point **: Center of the Bottom Bracket (BB) spindle center.
* **Saddle Position Reference (70mm Width Point)**: Saddles vary in shape, nose length, and tail flare. All saddle measurements are taken at the exact point where the saddle width measures **70 mm**.
  * **Saddle Height (Saddle Y)**: Measured along the seat tube axis from  to the top surface of the saddle at the 70mm width mark. Target tolerance: **±3 mm**.
  * **Saddle Setback (Saddle X)**: Horizontal millimeter distance from  to the 70mm width mark on the saddle (measured behind BB).
* **Cockpit Position Reference**:
  * **Cockpit Stack (Y)**: Vertical millimeter distance from  to the center of the handlebar clamp.
  * **Cockpit Reach (X)**: Horizontal millimeter distance from  to the center of the handlebar clamp.
  * **Saddle-to-Bar Drop**: Vertical difference .

## 2. The 4 Contact Planes Architecture (Pro-Level Fit Sequence)



### 2.1 Plane 1: Foot-Pedal Interface (Kufry & Obuv)
1. **Shoe Sizing & Width**: NEVER size up in length to accommodate wide feet. Measure foot length and foot width at metatarsals. Match 1:1 with wide-fit shoes (e.g. Lake CX238, Bont). Excess shoe length places cleat holes too far forward.
2. **Cleat Fore/Aft Setback**: Locate 1st metatarsal head (big toe ball) and 5th metatarsal head (little toe base). Set the pedal axle notch **10 to 17 mm behind the 1st metatarsal joint** (or fully rearward in cleat slots).
   * *Benefit*: Shortens the foot lever arm, offloading calf/Achilles strain and preserving quads and glutes over long rides.
3. **Insoles & Arch Support**: Throw away factory stock shoe liners. Use stiff medial arch-support insoles (e.g. SoulG8 or custom) to prevent arch collapse, foot splaying, and capillary/nerve squeezing.
4. **Stance Width / Q-Factor**: For wider pelvis or outward foot flare, push cleats inboard (shoes outward), add 1–2 mm pedal washers (max 2–4 mm/side), or use wide-axle pedals (+4 mm).

### 2.2 Plane 2: Saddle Ergonomics & Pelvis Foundation (Sedlo & Pánev)
1. **Cutout / Pressure Relief Channel**: Required for 90%+ of riders to prevent perineal/soft tissue compression, numbness, and pelvic tilting.
2. **Saddle Width**: Saddles wider than **145 mm** often rub inner thighs, forcing riders forward onto the narrow nose.
3. **Saddle Pitch (Sklon)**: **Level (0°) to maximum 1.5°–2.0° nose down** (measured on middle third of saddle).
   * *Danger (>2° down)*: Creates a slope where gravity pulls the rider forward, causing heavy bracing on arms/dlaně (numb hands).
4. **Saddle Setback & Weight Distribution**:
   * Moving saddle **rearward** shifts center of mass over pelvis, offloading weight from hands.
   * Moving saddle **forward** increases gravity load on front end and hands.

### 2.3 Plane 3: Cockpit & Upper Body (Kokpit & Řídítka)
1. **Handlebar Width (Drop Bars - Road/Gravel)**: Strictly 1:1 match to **Acromion Shoulder Width** (e.g. 380, 400, 420, 440 mm C-C).
   * *The 420mm Trap*: 90%+ of stock bikes ship with 420 mm bars. For 380 mm shoulders, a 420 mm bar forces outward elbow flare or wrist rolling, causing ulnar nerve compression (numb hands) and trapezius/neck strain.
2. **Shifter Hood Position**: Set shifters flat/level with drops vertical. Avoid excessive upward hood rotation as a cheat for long reach—this locks wrist angles and ruins drop usability.
3. **Saddle-to-Bar Drop Rules**:
   * Endurance / Low Flexibility: 0–50 mm drop.
   * Balanced: 40–70 mm drop.
   * Race / High Flexibility: 70–120 mm drop.
   * MTB / Trail / XC: -20 mm to +30 mm drop (Cockpit Stack often level with or higher than saddle).

### 2.4 Plane 4: Crank Length & Biomechanical Dynamics (Kliky)
1. **Crank Length Optimization (150–165 mm)**:
   * Shortening cranks (e.g. from 172.5 mm to 160–165 mm) reduces knee height at Top Dead Center by 7.5 mm.
   * Opens hip angle, prevents hip impingement and abdominal collision.
   * Allows raising saddle by 7.5 mm and running lower handlebars (aerodynamic drop) without crushing internal organs or closing hip angle.

## 3. Saddle-to-Bar Drop & Symptom Hard Guards

### 3.1 Hard Guards (Tvrdé Pojistky)
1. **NEVER advise "Lower Handlebars" (remove spacers) or "Move Saddle Forward" when:**
   * Rider reports **Numb Hands** (ulnar or median nerve compression).
   * Saddle pitch is steeper than -2° (nose down slope).
   * Rider style is Endurance or flexibility is low.
   * Factory spacer stack is unknown (must ask to verify current setup first!).
2. **NEVER advise "Raise Handlebars" when:**
   * Primary issue is anterior knee pain caused by low saddle (fix saddle Y first).
   * Rider has no symptoms and posture is stable.

### 3.2 Verification Protocol
* Replace command language ("Remove 20mm spacers") with **Diagnostic Verification**:
  * *"Check saddle pitch (0° to -1.5°). If level and hands still numb, check handlebar width vs. acromion. If width matches, shorten stem reach by 10–20 mm or add 10–15 mm spacers."*

4. **Hamstring & Pelvic Flexibility (Straight Leg Raise Test)**:
   * : Low flexibility -> require higher stack (+35 mm), shorter reach.
   * : Medium flexibility -> balanced stack (+20 mm).
   * : High flexibility -> race stack (+5 mm), higher drop capacity.
5. **Core Stability (Release Test)**:
   * Can rider release hands from hoods for 3 seconds at 20-min tempo without falling forward? If falls -> weight distribution too far forward.

### 7.2 Flat Bar Cockpit Ergonomics
Unlike Drop Bars (where width matches Acromion 1:1), Flat Bars require leverage for steering stability over technical obstacles:

1. **Flat Bar Width Target**:
   * **XC Race**:  (typically 720 – 760 mm).
   * **Trail / Enduro**:  (typically 760 – 800 mm).
   * *Biomechanics*: Excessively wide bars (>800 mm for average riders) over-rotate shoulders internally, locking elbows and creating heavy strain on the cervical spine and trapezius.
2. **Bar Sweep & Rotation**:
   * **Backsweep (7° – 9°)**: Pulls bar ends rearward toward the rider. Must align with natural wrist angle to prevent ulnar nerve compression.
   * **Upsweep (4° – 5°)**: Tilts bar ends slightly upward to support palm heel during aggressive cornering.
3. **Stem Length Alignment**:
   * Modern MTB pairs wide bars with short stems (**35 mm – 50 mm**).
   * *Rule*: For every 20 mm wider flat bar, shorten stem length by ~10 mm to maintain equivalent upper body reach.

### 7.3 Dropper Post Protocols
1. **Climbing / Full Extension Position**:
   * Set max extension Saddle Height (Saddle Y) for optimal 140°–145° knee extension angle at 6 o'clock pedal spindle.
2. **Descent / Technical Clearance**:
   * Dropper travel range (125 mm – 210 mm) allows full pelvic drop and body weight shift over rear wheel without saddle obstruction.

### 7.4 Modern Slack/Steep Geometry Adaptations
1. **Steep Seat Tube Angle (76° – 78°)**:
   * Keeps center of gravity forward over BB during steep climbs to prevent front wheel lift.
   * *Fitting Rule*: Physical saddle setback relative to BB center appears smaller ( behind BB) compared to road bikes (), but rider position relative to pedals remains anatomically correct due to mountain steepness.
2. **Slack Head Tube Angle (64° – 67°)**:
   * Pushes front wheel forward (long Front Center), requiring active weighted posture over handlebars during flat/uphill cornering.
