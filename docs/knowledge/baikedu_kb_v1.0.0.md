## 1. The myth of the bike weight (`bike-weight`)
Sources: "10 Things I Wish I Knew", "10 Years of Bike Maintenance". The weight is overrated because it is easy to read on a scale. Away from steep climbs one or two kilograms change the time of a ride very little; the kilometres ridden and the aerodynamics matter far more. Compliant tyres and a good riding position return more for the money than lighter components.
**Source videos:** CADE Media, "10 Things I Wish I Knew When I Started Cycling" (`jtw6JiprhS4`, 8:47, from 3:18; a second CADE Media upload with the same title exists, `lAoiGdenIZ0`, 11:46: check which one is meant); GCN Tech, "10 Years of Bike Maintenance Knowledge In 5 Minutes" (`igMGzFkj_y4`, from 3:42).

## 2. The 5-minute M-Check (`m-check`)
Source: `baikcare_kb_v1.1.0.md`, the M-Check protocol. The line of the letter M: front wheel and hub, headset and cockpit, bottom bracket and cranks, seatpost and saddle, rear wheel and derailleur. Look for play in the bearings, the state of the tyres, the tightness of the stem and seatpost bolts, the wear of the chain.
**Source video:** Cycling Weekly, "Do it yourself bicycle service | What you need to know | Cycling Weekly" (`Iuy7wwMm6HY`, from 0:25).

## 3. Numb hands and nerve flossing (`numb-hands`)
Sources: `baikfit_kb_v1.4.0.md`, the nerve glides video of Rehab Science (`ktOw-6irlYs`). Numbness in the little finger (and half of the ring finger) points to the ulnar nerve, in the thumb, index and middle finger to the median nerve. Usual causes: handlebars that are too wide, a long reach, a saddle tilted down. Nerve flossing: a gentle alternating movement of arms and head slides the nerve in its sheath and restores circulation, without aggressive stretching. General information; a doctor or physiotherapist for numbness that stays.
**Source videos:** SEMIPRO CYCLING, "Stop Numb Hands While Cycling With This Quick Fix" (`OdSrPA1Mh8U`, from 4:23); Bikefit James, "8 reasons you get NUMB HANDS when cycling" (`sJV4WS-5Q-M`, from 0:15).

## 4. Shoes, arch support and cleat position (`cleats`)
Sources: "10 Easy Fixes for a Perfect Bike Fit" (Lee Prescott: factory insoles are only for trying the shoe on in the shop), "5 Tips For The Perfect Bike Fit" (James: sizing up for width moves the cleat off the joints, too far forward). A cleat further back, between the first and the fifth metatarsal, eases the calves, steadies the ankle and brings in the glutes and the hamstrings (the source of this last sentence was not named by the owner).

## 5. Fuelling on the bike (`fuelling`)
Sources: "20 Things I WISH I Knew" (eat and drink from the first half hour; hunger means glycogen is already falling), "BEGINNER CYCLIST to INTERMEDIATE" (a whole-day ratio of about 60 per cent carbohydrates, 20 per cent protein, 20 per cent fat). The ratio is a whole-day figure for a rider building up, not a ratio for the food taken during a ride. General information, not dietary advice.
**Source videos:** tristantakevideo, "20 Things I WISH I Knew as a Beginner Road Cyclist" (`0_qO8ki_PPg`, from 1:34); tristantakevideo, "BEGINNER CYCLIST to INTERMEDIATE: 8 Tips to ride FASTER, for LONGER" (`UHBOeddOzB4`, from 2:32).

## 6. The science of tyre pressure and width (`tyre-science`)
Source: `baikcare_kb_v1.1.0.md` section 5a and "Wider Tyres Aren't Faster. Here's What Is." (The Aero Hour, `AG4N5zeTSw4`). Lab: a wider tyre at the same pressure rolls easier; on the road the same squash and comfort give about the same rolling resistance. Over-inflated tyres bounce and lose watts to impedance; a wider tyre allows a lower pressure without a pinch flat. Rule of 105: the rim about 5 per cent wider than the measured tyre.
**Source video:** The Aero Hour, "Wider Tyres Aren't Faster. Here's What Is." (`AG4N5zeTSw4`, from 0:40).

## 7. Proper gear shifting technique and drivetrain care (`shifting-technique-drivetrain-care`)
Topic `#maintenance`, 3 minutes, leads to bAIkcare. **Source video:** Global Cycling Network, "How To Shift Your Gears Correctly" (`dl1ZJsoqvhY`, 9:27, from 3:48; the id in the brief, `fT305L6l954`, does not exist on YouTube (oEmbed 404); the video found by channel and title is used; its chapters "How to use bike gears properly" (3:40), "Change your bike gears early", "Smooth gear changes" and "Avoid cross-chaining" match the facts below, the numbers were not checked).
* The right shifter moves the rear cassette (fine adjustment), the left shifter the front chainring (big jumps). Holds for a bike with two chainrings.
* Keep a steady cadence of about 80 to 95 rpm; the gear follows the terrain.
* Anticipate a climb and shift down about 20 metres before it, so as not to shift under full load.
* Ease the pressure on the pedals for a moment while shifting.
* Avoid cross-chaining (big-big, small-small): it wears the drivetrain parts.
