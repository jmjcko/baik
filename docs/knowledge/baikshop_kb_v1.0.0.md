## 2. The Price-to-Performance Sweet Spot (£1,000–£2,000 / 30,000–60,000 CZK)

### 2.1 The Diminishing Returns Curve in Bicycle Purchasing
Empirical analysis of bicycle market data reveals a steep inflection point along the cost-benefit curve:

* **Entry Bracket (<£800 / <24,000 CZK):** Severe component compromises (heavy wire-bead tires, unsealed bearings, mechanical disc brakes with poor modulation).
* **The Sweet Spot (£1,000–£2,000 / 30,000–60,000 CZK):**
  - Premium aluminum alloy frames (e.g. Specialized Allez Sprint, Trek Emonda ALR, Canyon Endurace AL) or value carbon frames.
  - Reliable hydraulic disc brakes (Shimano Tiagra / 105 R7100 / SRAM Apex).
  - Modern clearance for 32–35mm tires.
  - Delivers **92% of the dynamic performance** of an £8,000 WorldTour superbike.
* **Superbike Bracket (>£5,000 / >130,000 CZK):** Each additional £1,000 yields less than 0.5% in speed gains and marginal weight reductions (sub-7.5 kg), with sharply increased repair and proprietary replacement costs.

## 4. Scientific Tire Pressure Modeling (Silca / SRAM Impedance Model)

### 4.1 Surface Roughness & The Impedance Breakpoint
* **Historic Fallacy:** "Higher pressure = harder tire = faster roll." This is only true on glass-smooth velodrome wooden boards.
* **The Impedance Breakpoint:** On real asphalt, as tire pressure climbs beyond the optimal breakpoint, the tire ceases to deform over microscopic surface asperities. Instead, the entire bicycle and rider mass vibrates upward and backward, causing catastrophic kinetic energy loss (internal vibration damping by human soft tissue).
* **Optimal Pressure Rule:** Always run pressure slightly below the calculated impedance breakpoint rather than above it. Lower pressure delivers equal or lower rolling resistance with exponentially greater cornering grip and spinal comfort.

### 4.2 Core Calculation Input Parameters

$$\text{Tire Pressure} = f(\text{Total System Weight}, \text{Effective Measured Tire Width}, \text{Rim Internal Width}, \text{Surface Category}, \text{Casing TPI})$$

* **Total System Weight:** Rider weight (kg) + Bicycle weight (kg) + Gear/Water/Bottle weight (kg).
* **Weight Distribution:** 42% on front tire, 58% on rear tire for standard endurance road geometry.
* **Surface Coefficients:**
  - Smooth New Asphalt: Benchmark 1.00
  - Rough Coarse Asphalt / Chip-seal: Deduct **8–12 PSI**
  - Packed Dirt / Fine Gravel: Deduct **15–20 PSI**
  - Wet Conditions: Deduct **5–7 PSI** to maximize contact patch friction
